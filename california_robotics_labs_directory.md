## 1. STANFORD UNIVERSITY

**Main Website:** https://src.stanford.edu/
**Location:** Stanford, CA 94305

### Stanford Robotics Center (SRC)

Member labs span across departments of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Bioengineering, Bio-X, Civil and Environmental Engineering, Computer Science, Electrical Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, and Radiology.

### Robotics Labs:

#### Stanford Robotics Lab (Manipulation Lab)
- **Director:** Prof. Oussama Khatib
- **Focus:** Robotic manipulation and control, human-robot interaction in unstructured environments
- **Website:** https://cs.stanford.edu/group/manips/ | https://manips.sites.stanford.edu/
- **Department:** Computer Science

#### IPRL (Interactive Perception and Robot Learning Lab)
- **Focus:** Robust sensorimotor coordination, autonomous manipulation tasks, intersection of robotics, ML, and computer vision
- **Website:** https://iprl.stanford.edu/

#### REAL (Robotics and Embodied Artificial Intelligence Lab)
- **Director:** Prof. Jiajun Wu
- **Focus:** Algorithms for intelligent systems learning from physical world interactions
- **Website:** https://real.stanford.edu/
- **Department:** Computer Science

#### Aerospace Robotics Lab (ARL)
- **Department:** Aeronautics and Astronautics
- **Focus:** Feedback control, integrated sensing systems, task-level autonomy
- **Website:** https://web.stanford.edu/group/arl/

#### Navigation and Autonomous Vehicles (NAV) Lab
- **Focus:** Robust positioning, navigation, timing technologies, navigation safety, cyber security, and resilience
- **Research Areas:** GPS/GNSS, autonomous navigation

#### Assistive Robotics and Manipulation Lab (ARM Lab)
- **Focus:** Robots that improve everyday life by anticipating and acting on human needs

#### Biomechatronics Lab
- **Director:** Prof. Steve Collins
- **Focus:** Wearable robotic devices to improve efficiency, speed, and balance of walking and running, especially for people with disabilities
- **Applications:** Prosthetics, exoskeletons

#### CHARM Lab (Collaborative Haptics and Robotics in Medicine)
- **Director:** Prof. Allison Okamura
- **Department:** Mechanical Engineering
- **Focus:** Safe and intuitive haptic interfaces for enhanced physical connection in remote and virtual interaction
- **Website:** https://charm.stanford.edu/
- **Applications:** Medical robotics, surgical systems

#### Movement Lab
- **Focus:** Coordinated, functional, and efficient whole-body movements for digital agents and real robots

#### Salisbury Robotics Lab
- **Focus:** Various robotics projects across five main categories
- **Research Areas:** Manipulation, sensing, control

#### ILIAD
- **Focus:** Algorithms for autonomous systems that safely and reliably interact with people
- **Affiliation:** Stanford AI Lab

#### CIFE (Center for Integrated Facility Engineering)
- **Focus:** Architecture, Engineering, Construction, and Operations (AECO) industry
- **Applications:** Construction robotics, automation

**Contact for Outreach:**
Stanford Education & STEM Outreach: https://eso.stanford.edu/

## HOW TO CONTACT THESE LABS

### For High School Partnerships:

#### Direct Contact Methods:

1. **Visit Lab Websites** - Most have contact forms or faculty email addresses
2. **Look for "Outreach" or "Education" Pages** - Many labs have dedicated K-12 programs
3. **Contact Lab Directors** - Email format usually: firstname.lastname@university.edu
4. **Reach Out to University STEM Outreach Offices:**
   - **Stanford:** eso.stanford.edu
   - **UC Berkeley:** Lawrence Hall of Science (lawrencehallofscience.org)
   - **Caltech:** Through CAST center
   - **UCLA:** Samueli School Outreach
   - **UCSD:** Contextual Robotics Institute
   - **UC Riverside:** robotics.ucr.edu/engagement/k-12-outreach

#### Indirect Contact Methods:

5. **Attend Robotics Competitions** - Where university teams mentor (FIRST Robotics, VEX, RoboSub)
6. **Apply to Formal Summer Programs** - Many universities offer structured high school programs
7. **Leverage Alumni Connections** - High school alumni working in robotics can facilitate introductions
8. **Connect at Science Fairs** - University faculty often judge regional/state competitions

### Best Practices for Outreach:

**Timing:**
- Email in **September-October** for following summer programs
- Applications typically due **December-January**
- Program decisions released **March-April**
- Programs run **June-August**

**Email Content:**
- Mention specific research interests that align with lab focus
- Reference specific papers or projects from the lab
- Highlight underrepresented background if applicable (many programs prioritize diversity)
- Be specific about what you hope to learn and contribute
- Include relevant experience (courses, projects, competitions)

**For Schools (not individual students):**
- Propose mutual benefits (community service hours for college students, research subjects, diversity outreach)
- Start with small asks (lab tour, guest lecture) before proposing formal partnerships
- Identify champion faculty member who values K-12 outreach
- Prepare to commit 2-3 years minimum for meaningful partnership

### Priority Student Populations:

Many labs prioritize:
- First-generation college students
- Underrepresented minorities in STEM
- Low-income students
- Students with limited STEM access outside school
- Female students in engineering/robotics

### Partnership Models:

1. **Summer Research Internships** (6-12 weeks, individual students)
2. **Academic Year Mentorship** (9+ months, remote or in-person)
3. **School-University Research Partnerships** (multi-year, cohort-based)
4. **FIRST Robotics Mentorship** (competition season, team-based)
5. **Teacher Professional Development** (building school capacity)
6. **Lab Tours and Demonstrations** (one-time engagement)
